MECHANICAL DESIGN · PRESSURE COMPONENTS

Flow Tee

Mechanical design of a compact flow-routing component integrating multiple tubing and threaded interfaces while maintaining clear internal flow paths and manufacturable geometry.

Fusion 360 Mechanical Design DFM Technical Drawings FEA

OVERVIEW

Routing flow through a compact mechanical body.

The Flow Tee was developed as part of a larger wellhead equipment system and provides the transition between the primary vertical flow path and horizontal production-line connections.

The design required multiple intersecting internal passages, tubing interfaces, threaded ports, and sufficient material surrounding the flow bores for structural integrity.

I developed the component geometry in Fusion 360 and produced the associated technical drawing with orthographic and sectional views used to communicate the internal geometry.

DESIGN REQUIREMENTS

Geometry driven by interfaces and flow routing.

01

Primary Flow Path

Maintain a continuous vertical passage through the main body while integrating cross-flow connections.

02

Side Connections

Integrate threaded lateral ports for connection to supporting flow or instrumentation hardware.

03

Tubing Interfaces

Incorporate defined tubing connection geometry at the upper and lower ends of the component.

04

Manufacturability

Balance internal passages, external geometry, wall thickness, and machining access within a compact body.

MY ROLE

CAD development and engineering definition.

I created the 3D component geometry in Fusion 360, developing the main cylindrical body, intersecting internal bores, side-port bosses, threaded connections, and transition features.

Particular attention was required at the intersections between internal passages because these regions affect both flow continuity and the amount of material available around each opening.

I then translated the final geometry into a detailed engineering drawing with section views used to define the internal features that are not visible from the exterior CAD model.

CAD DEVELOPMENT

Intersecting passages inside a single machined body.

01

Vertical Main Bore

Forms the primary flow path through the component and connects the upper and lower tubing interfaces.

02

Cross-Flow Passage

Redirects fluid from the main vertical bore toward a horizontal production connection.

03

Threaded Auxiliary Port

Provides a smaller connection interface integrated directly into the main body.

04

Machined Transitions

Fillets and local geometry transitions reduce abrupt section changes while maintaining machining feasibility.

ENGINEERING DRAWING

Defining geometry that cannot be seen externally.

The technical drawing uses multiple section views to fully describe the intersecting internal passages.

Section A-A defines the horizontal cross-flow geometry and threaded side connections, while Section B-B defines the vertical bore and upper and lower tubing interfaces.

This drawing work was important because the functional geometry of the component exists primarily inside the body rather than on its external surfaces.

MECHANICAL INTERFACES

Multiple connection standards in one component.

MAIN CONNECTION

2-7/8" EUE

Upper and lower geometry designed to accept defined tubing connection interfaces.

SIDE PORT

NPT Thread

Threaded side-port geometry integrated directly into the main cross-flow body.

AUXILIARY PORT

Secondary NPT Interface

Smaller threaded connection incorporated below the primary horizontal flow passage.

STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS

Connecting geometry with finite element analysis.

Intersecting bores and threaded ports create local changes in wall thickness and potential stress-concentration regions.

Structural finite element analysis can be used to evaluate these regions under internal pressure and identify areas where geometry refinement may be required.

This allows the CAD model to be evaluated not only for fit and manufacturability, but also for structural behavior before the design progresses further.
